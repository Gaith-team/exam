The Big Picture

Common causes of bleeding in early pregnancy: Abortion (miscarriage), Ectopic pregnancy, Hydatidiform (vesicular) mole, or cervical malignancy.

28 wksAbortion = termination before fetal viability
24–28weeks: fetal viability window (with incubator)
10–20%of clinical pregnancies miscarry
>95%of ectopics implant in the fallopian tube
1

Abortion

Bleeding + cervix status + pain decide the type. Most occur in the 2nd–3rd month.
2

Ectopic

Pregnancy outside the uterine cavity. Danger: rupture → hypovolemic shock.
3

Vesicular mole

Abnormal tissue instead of a baby. Danger: choriocarcinoma, pre-eclampsia.
Exam angle: Always connect bleeding → shock → nursing priorities: vital signs, IV fluids, oxygen, supine position with a pillow under head and shoulder, psychological support.

Diagnosis · Complications · Treatment

Diagnostic tests

  • Pregnancy test
  • Ultrasound — safest and confirmatory; shows if products of conception are intact

Complications

  • Shock
  • Infection → death or infertility
  • DIC
  • Depression, hysteria, grief

Treatment

  • Antibiotics with surgical abortion
  • Manual/electric vacuum aspiration up to 12–14 wks
  • D&C = obsolete method

Other Types

Habitual

≥ 3 successive spontaneous abortions. Commonest treatable cause: incompetent internal cervical os.

Therapeutic

Legal termination by a physician for a medical indication.

Criminal

Illegal termination with no medical or obstetric indication.

Ectopic Pregnancy

Any pregnancy occurring outside the uterine cavity — commonly “tubal pregnancy”.

Vesicular Mole

Also called gestational trophoblastic disease. Rare; abnormal tissue grows instead of a baby; may be cancerous.

Complete mole

46 chromosomes

No baby ever develops; placenta grows many fluid-filled cysts.
Partial mole

69 chromosomes

Two sperms fertilize one ovum; baby starts but cannot survive.
